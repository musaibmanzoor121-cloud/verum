# Verum — Engine A Fairness Report

> Generated by `python -m eval.fairness`. It checks whether some *kinds* of genuine applicants are flagged more than others — especially **non-native English writers**, the group AI detectors are documented to harm most.

## Why this report exists

In 2023 a Stanford study found that popular GPT detectors misclassified a majority of TOEFL essays written by non-native English speakers as AI-generated, while rarely misclassifying essays by native speakers. A screening tool with that bias would quietly penalize international applicants. Verum's answer is to **measure the disparity and guard against it in CI**, not to assume it away.

The metric is the **false-positive rate** at the product threshold (**0.40**): of the genuine humans in each group, what fraction does Engine A wrongly flag as AI?

## Honest reading of this result

- **The non-native-English group is not disproportionately flagged** — its false-positive rate matches the rest (and its mean score sits among the lowest). This is the specific bias the report was built to catch, and `tests/test_eval_gate.py` fails CI if a future change ever flags one of these samples.
- **The closest-to-threshold humans are the *polished* writers** (em-dashes, rule-of-three) — the honest overlap zone. Verum keeps them under the line on this set, but they are where risk lives, so they route to a human rather than being auto-cleared with false confidence.
- **The misses are the lightly-humanized AI samples** (see the recall table and eval/ROBUSTNESS.md) — a deliberate trade: Verum would rather miss edited AI than accuse a real person.
- **Small sample.** These are directional rates on a handful of examples per group, not certified population figures. Expand the set with real, consented samples to publish defensible numbers.
